Preparation, and what to leave alone

You do not need to fill cracks, strip paper, wash the outside or buy dust sheets before you ask. That work is the decorating. On an interior painting job, floors, furniture and fittings are covered before a tin is opened. Holes and cracks are filled and sanded, stains are spot-primed, and doors, frames, skirting and banisters are cut in to a clean line. The room is left tidy.

Leave flaking woodwork where it is, and point it out. If paper is to come off, say so rather than stripping it yourself unless that is already agreed. The wall has to be dry. Damp paper stains and lets go. Ceiling and wall repairs make cracks, holes and scarred plaster good for paint and paper. They are not a replaster, and they are not a damp-proof course. If a wall is wet, a new coat will not fix the cause. Clear what you would mind getting a speck on, and say which furniture cannot be moved.

Inside an Enniskillen house

Town houses here often mean stairs: a narrow hall, a turn, and a landing repainted around the edges for years. Spindles and the high wall over the stairs take longer than a square bedroom, so put them on the quote. Chanterhill, Hillview, Derrychara and the edges of town are the other regular brief: tired rooms, yellowed woodwork, and ceilings with old filler spots.

Matt is usual on walls. A wipeable eggshell or satin is usual on doors, skirting and banisters. Kitchens and bathrooms want a finish you can clean. A dark colour going pale, or a water stain, needs a primer and more coats. Light in Fermanagh is flat for much of the year, and a colour from a phone often dies on a north-facing wall, so test a sample in the morning and again in the evening. Wallpapering for a hall, a stairs or one feature wall waits until the wall is sound, dry and flat. The services page lists all five kinds of work if the house needs more than paint.

Rooms do not share the outside calendar. Interior painting can go ahead when the house is heated and the surfaces are dry. A cold spare room is a reason to wait.

The outside, and a week by the loughs

The outside waits on the weather. Render and older stone stay damp, algae sits on shaded walls, and a coat put on too soon fails. Lake air is not a reason to put interior emulsion on an outside wall. Exterior painting covers rendered walls, pebbledash, masonry, fascias, soffits, bargeboards, doors, windows and garden walls. Name which of those you want. “The outside” can mean the front door, or the whole shell, and say whether the old coat is peeling or only tired.

He looks at the wall first, and he would rather wait than trap water in a wet or crumbling surface. Green growth is washed off, loose paint is scraped, and bare patches are primed. Late spring through early autumn is the realistic window. Winter is often the wrong week. Soft timber will not be restored by paint. He is a decorator, not a roofer.
