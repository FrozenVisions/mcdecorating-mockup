How to choose a painter and decorator in Omagh

What to ask before booking a painter and decorator in Omagh: written quotes, preparation, coats, materials, insurance and the Northern Ireland weather.

Choosing a painter and decorator in Omagh starts with the written quote, not with whoever answered first. A recommendation or a page online can be a fair start. Neither says what the price includes. This is a homeowner’s guide to the questions worth asking before you book anyone. The Omagh page is separate: it is what MC Decorating does in the town, where Martin Kelly is based.

What to ask a painter and decorator in Omagh before you book

Ask for the quote in writing, with the rooms and the surfaces named. A single number is not something you can compare.

  • Preparation. Filling, sanding, priming stains, washing growth off an outside wall and scraping loose paint are what make a finish last. If that work is not on the quote, it is not in the price.
  • Number of coats. Ask how many are included, and whether a primer is allowed for when a dark colour is going pale or a stain might show through. Ordinary emulsion over a water stain lets the mark come back.
  • Paint brand and finish, chosen with you. Matt on walls, and a wipeable eggshell or satin on doors and skirting, is the usual split. Kitchens and bathrooms need a finish you can clean. Agree it before the job, or name the tin if you have already bought the paint.
  • Who supplies the materials. Say whether paint, filler and primer are in the price or left for you to buy. A lower figure with the materials excluded is a different job.
  • Timescales. Ask for a start, how many days, and what happens if rain or a damp patch stops the work. Stairs and landings take longer than a bedroom, so put them on the quote if they are part of it.
  • Tidy-up. Floors, furniture and fittings covered before a tin is opened, and the room left tidy. On a longer job, that includes the hall, because sanding dust travels.
  • Public liability insurance. Ask to see the certificate. A spoken yes is not the document. Ask anyone you might book, including Martin. This article does not state what cover he holds.

Also ask who will be in the house. A decorator in Omagh may work alone or bring someone else for a larger job. The quote should name who is responsible.

Getting quotes you can compare

Two painters in Omagh can both be capable and still price different jobs. One may have allowed for filling and woodwork. The other may have priced the walls only. The lower figure is not a saving until the descriptions match.

Give each person the same brief: the town, the rooms, whether paper comes off, whether woodwork is included, and anything cracked, stained or damp. If only one of them has visited, they have not seen the same thing.

Interior and exterior timing in Northern Ireland weather

Inside and outside do not share a calendar. Winters here are mild and wet.

Interior painting can go ahead in a Tyrone winter when the house is heated and the surfaces are dry. A cold spare room, or a ceiling that is still clammy, is a reason to wait. Paint will not hide mould that condensation is still feeding. Mould and condensation before painting is how to tell that from a leak.

Exterior painting needs a dry surface and time for the coat to cure. Omagh sits under the Sperrins, and a wall can look dry at lunchtime while it still holds last night’s rain. Late spring through early autumn is the realistic window. Exterior paint for damp Northern Ireland weather covers when to leave a wall alone. If the quote says the outside work moves with the forecast, do not book a week that is already forecast wet.

What to have ready

You do not need to fill, strip or buy dust sheets before a price. That work is the decorating. Have the town, the rooms, a rough date, and whether paper is coming off. Point out a stain or a damp patch, and do not paint over it the night before.

Once you have booked, preparing a room before the decorator arrives covers what to move and what to leave. Name the tin if you have chosen a colour.

The words “house painter Omagh” on a van do not say whether ceilings and woodwork are included. Ask. A silent ceiling, or doors left out, is a different job.

Red flags

  • No written quote, or a price that will not say what is included.
  • Pressure to pay a large cash sum, or to book the same day, before you have read it.
  • A refusal to show public liability insurance when you ask to see the certificate.
  • Painting over mould, a wet patch or flaking render because the diary is free. Paint does not dry a wall or mend a leak.
  • A round figure with no preparation, coats or materials.
  • No name on the quote, so you cannot tell who is responsible.

A missing shopfront is not one of those flags. You want a name on the quote and a description you can compare.

How to choose, then ask for a price

Take the written quote that describes the same rooms, the same preparation and the same finish, from someone who will show an insurance certificate and name a start. The lowest number only wins when it is the same work, in Omagh or further across Tyrone and Fermanagh.
