What landlord painting and decorating Omagh includes

The quote comes after he knows the rooms, the condition and the town. Say how many rooms, whether paper has to come off, and the date the next tenant is due. The preparation is the same as a home. Hard-wearing finishes on doors, frames and the lower walls that take the knocks are how a rental turnaround is approached. Preparation you do not need to start

You do not need to fill cracks, strip paper, wash walls or buy dust sheets before you ask. That work is the decorating. On an interior painting job, floors, furniture and fittings are covered before a tin is opened. Holes and cracks are filled and sanded, stains are spot-primed, and doors, frames, skirting and banisters are cut in to a clean line. The room is left tidy.

Leave flaking woodwork where it is, and point it out. If paper has to come off, say so, and leave the stripping unless that has already been agreed. Old paper comes off only where the wall is dry. Damp paper stains and lets go. Wallpapering for a hall, a stairs or one feature wall waits until the wall is sound, dry and flat. Ceiling and wall repairs make cracks, holes and scarred plaster good for paint and paper. They are not a replaster, and they are not a damp-proof course. If a wall is wet, a new coat will not fix the cause, and paint will not hide mould that condensation is still feeding. Clear what you would mind getting a speck on, and say which furniture cannot be moved.

Rooms that take the knocks

The job that comes up most in Omagh is the hall, stairs and landing. It is the part every visitor sees, and it takes the knocks. Wallpaper above a dado, woodwork that has gone yellow, and spindles that have never had a clean finish are the usual brief. Spindles and the high wall over the stairs take longer than a square bedroom, so put them on the quote. Ceilings with old filler spots are common in town houses that have been patched. Newer estates, where the plaster is still moving, are the other side of the same town. Say which sort of house it is.

Matt is the usual choice for walls. A wipeable eggshell or satin is the usual choice for doors, skirting and banisters. Kitchens and bathrooms want a finish that can be cleaned. A dark colour going pale, or a water stain, takes a primer and more coats, and that belongs in the quote. If the paint is already bought, give the name of the tin.

Interior painting can go ahead when the house is heated and the surfaces are dry. A cold spare room, or a ceiling that is still clammy, is a reason to wait.

The outside, and a rental that is not in the town

Exterior work waits for a dry spell. On the edge of Omagh, towards the Sperrins, outside walls take more weather. If the wall is still holding rain, it does not get a coat that week. A wall can look dry at lunchtime and still be holding last night’s rain. Late spring through early autumn is the realistic window. Winter is often the wrong week. Exterior painting covers rendered walls, pebbledash, masonry, fascias, soffits, bargeboards, doors, windows and garden walls. Name which you want. “The outside” can mean the front door, or the whole shell.

He looks at the wall first, and he would rather wait than trap water in a wet or crumbling surface. If the timber has gone soft, paint will not restore it. He is a decorator, not a roofer.
