Mould and condensation marks: what to do before painting a ceiling or wall

Why mould and condensation marks on an interior ceiling or wall come back if you paint over them, and what to check first in a damp Omagh winter.

Get a free quote Call 07907 588 541

10 November 2026

Black marks on a bathroom ceiling, a grey bloom behind a wardrobe, and rings in a kitchen corner show up every winter in Omagh and across Tyrone and Fermanagh. The weather is mild and wet, windows stay shut, and cold outside walls never really warm through. Paint over the marks and they come back through the new coat, or the growth sits on top of it. This is about inside ceilings and walls. Wet outside walls are a different job, set out in exterior paint for damp Northern Ireland weather.

Martin paints interiors when the surface is dry and the cause has been dealt with.

Why a coat does not hide it

The mark is water, or growth on that water, not a colour a coat will hide. Ordinary emulsion lets staining through, so a ceiling that looked white on Saturday can show the ring again within a few weeks. Primer on a wet patch fails the same way: it blisters, or the stain returns when the room steams up. Cold ceilings, outside bedroom walls and the plaster above a shower stay cold when the house is left unheated. Paint does not warm them.

Condensation, or water getting in

Most winter marks in bathrooms, kitchens and bedrooms are condensation. Steam from showers, cooking and drying clothes lands on the coldest surface. It is usually condensation when the marks are in several cold places, the surface is dry to the touch by the afternoon, it eases when a window is opened, and no single patch is growing from one point.

A leak, penetrating damp or rising damp needs the right trade before a decorator starts.

  • A leak stays wet, or worsens after rain, a shower upstairs, or a pipe running. A ring from one spot, or a drip, is a plumber’s job, or a roofer’s if the water is coming through the roof.
  • Penetrating damp is rain getting through a gable, a chimney, a gutter or a sill. The patch follows the weather. Fix the outside fault before any inside coat.
  • Rising damp sits low on a ground-floor wall. Skirtings soften, plaster bubbles up from the floor, and there is often a tide mark. Emulsion will not stop it. A decorator does not install a damp-proof course.

Ceiling and wall repairs on this site are making good for decoration once the plaster is fit to paint, not a damp survey. If the mould covers a large area, or you are worried about health, ask your GP, your landlord, or environmental health.

Habits that keep the moisture down

  • Run the bathroom and kitchen fan during the steam and after you leave. A tissue at the grille should pull in. A fan blowing into the loft, not outside, only moves the damp.
  • Leave trickle vents open. Painting those slots shut, or covering them with curtains, blocks the air the windows were fitted to give.
  • Drying clothes in a shut bedroom loads that room with water. Open a window if you have to, keep wardrobes off the cold wall, and vent a tumble dryer outside.
  • Steady background heat keeps walls warmer than a cold house blasted for two hours at night.
  • Leave a gap behind furniture on an external wall. That stops the black patch in the shape of a headboard.

Clean it, and do not mix products

If a decorator is coming, say there is mould or a stain and leave the washing to the job. The note on preparing a room before the decorator arrives already says not to wash walls with something you would not put on your hands, and not to paint a damp patch the day before.

On a small patch of your own, wear gloves, open the window, and follow the label on a cleaner sold for mould on paint. Do not mix products. Bleach and another cleaner together can give off dangerous fumes in a bathroom. Wipe, rather than dry-sanding, and leave flaking paint so the plaster stays put.

Let it dry, then block the stain

In a Tyrone winter a bathroom ceiling can take days to dry. If it feels clammy, or the ring is still dark and damp, wait. Keep the room heated as usual and the steam down.

Water stains and the ghost of mould still show through ordinary emulsion, so a stain-blocking primer for interior walls and ceilings goes on the dry patch, then the finish. That is a type of product, not a brand tied to this work. It will not hold a stain while water is still getting in.

Paint that suits the room

A bathroom or kitchen wants an emulsion labelled for those rooms, moisture-resistant or mould-resistant. Bedroom matt on a shower ceiling stays damper and marks sooner. Other rooms can take a normal interior emulsion once the cause has gone. A mould-resistant tin resists growth on a clean, dry surface. It does not mend a leak, a blocked gutter or a fan that does not extract.

Ceilings show every tide mark, so they are where the primer earns its place. If the plaster is soft, hollow or coming away, stop. Interior painting is for a surface that can take paint. Where it cannot, ceiling and wall repairs come first.

When it is a decorator’s job

Clean and touch in a small dry patch yourself if you can reach it. Ask a decorator when the marks run across a ceiling, the paint is flaking, wallpaper has lifted, or you want the room finished after a leak has been repaired. Say which room, whether anything is still wet, and whether a plumber or roofer has already been.

Name the town, whether that is Omagh or further out in Enniskillen or Strabane. Use the quote form and send a photograph of the stain on WhatsApp. The colour can wait until the surface is dry.
