On the exterior painting page the surfaces are rendered walls, pebbledash, masonry, fascias, soffits, bargeboards, doors, windows and garden walls. A note that only says “the outside” can mean the front door, or it can mean the whole shell. Name which of those you want, and say whether the old coat is peeling or only tired.

The order of the work does not change with the town. He looks at the wall first. Paint will not hold on a wet or crumbling surface, and he would rather wait than trap water in it. Washing down includes the green growth on shaded and north-facing walls. Loose paint is scraped, cracks are filled, and bare patches are primed. Render and pebbledash take a masonry paint suited to them. Exterior wood takes its own system. Paths, planting and glass are protected, and the place is left tidy.

Emulsion from a kitchen or a bedroom is not an outside paint. The Enniskillen page says the same of the lough shore: lake air is not a reason to put interior emulsion on an outside wall. The longer account of wet walls, algae and soft timber is exterior paint for damp Northern Ireland weather.

Irvinestown is about twenty-five minutes from Omagh, the first Fermanagh town on the road to Enniskillen. Enniskillen is about forty minutes down the A32, through Irvinestown. The quote includes that travel. It is not priced as if the house were a street in Omagh. Render and older stone stay damp, algae sits on shaded walls, and a coat put on too soon fails. The outside work is planned around a dry spell, the same as on the Sperrin side of Tyrone.

Kesh is the village on the north shore of Lower Lough Erne, out through Ederney. It is about half an hour to forty minutes from Martin, depending on the townland. Gables face the lough, north walls go green, and fascia boards soften at the ends. If the timber has gone soft, paint will not restore it. He will point that out. Replacing rotten wood is a separate decision: he is a decorator, not a roofer. Masonry wants a breathable exterior paint, and only once the wall is actually dry.

Lisnaskea is the furthest town on the list, past Enniskillen towards Upper Lough Erne. Allow about an hour from Omagh. Exterior wood and render take the same lough weather, sometimes with fewer neighbours to shelter a gable. Peeling paint is scraped back. A fresh coat over flakes will not last the winter. A full house, a shop, or a place that has been waiting is the sort of job that makes the drive sensible. A tiny touch-up may not, and he would rather say so than book a day that does not work. When the week is the wrong week

Late spring through early autumn is the realistic window for outside work. Winter is often the wrong week even when the diary is free. Omagh sits under the Sperrins, and Enniskillen, Kesh and the lough shore stay damp. A wall can look dry at lunchtime and still be holding last night’s rain. A wet wall, a crumbling surface, frost on a fresh coat, or an active leak above the paint are reasons to wait.

You do not need to wash the walls, scrape the flakes or buy the masonry paint before you ask. That work is the decorating. Point out what is peeling, what is only faded, and any timber that feels soft.

Rooms do not share that calendar. Interior painting can go ahead when the house is heated and the surfaces are dry. A cold spare room is still a reason to wait. The outside waits on the weather either way.
