A finish that can take the knocks

A hall is handled more than a spare bedroom. Bags, coats and children meet the lower wall, the panelling, the doors and the rail. A wipeable eggshell or satin is the practical choice on those surfaces. Matt still suits the upper wall, which is looked at more than it is knocked. Agree that split before the work starts. A hard-wearing finish here means one that can be wiped. It does not mean a named product.

Drying times are general. They depend on the tin, the heat in the house, and how often the front door opens. In a heated house, water-based paint on a wall is often touch-dry in a few hours, and a further coat commonly waits until later the same day or the next morning. Eggshell or satin on spindles and the handrail usually wants longer between coats than the walls. A hallway open to the street stays cooler, and a Tyrone winter slows it.
