The useful preparation, before a decorator comes to a house in Omagh or elsewhere in Tyrone and Fermanagh, is ordinary household work. It is not filling cracks, stripping paper, or deciding whether a damp wall can take paint. Those belong to the job. This is how to leave the room so the first morning is spent on the walls.

Leave the walls to the decorating

Filling, sanding, priming stains, and stripping paper that is already failing are decorating, not a task to finish the night before. The order for paper is in how walls are prepared for wallpaper. Cracks and holes are ceiling and wall repairs. A wet outside wall is a different question, set out in exterior paint for damp Northern Ireland weather. You do not need to start any of that yourself.

Leave old paint where it is. Flaking woodwork in an older house in Omagh, Fintona or Enniskillen can be decades old. Do not sand it, burn it off, or scrape it back to bare wood. Dust and heat are not a head start, and a chemical stripper is the wrong thing to open in a bedroom. Point the woodwork out when you ask for the quote. Making it good is part of the decorating.

Clear what you care about, and what is in the way

Clear the surfaces of anything you would mind getting a speck on: photographs, ornaments, clocks, lamps, medicines, and paper on a windowsill. Curtains and blinds can stay. If you want them down so a frame can be painted, take them down only when the fittings lift off easily. Do not unscrew a pelmet or a blind that needs a ladder. Say it is still up.

Rugs, toy boxes, cables and small furniture come out of the room, or into the middle if that is the only space. A heavy wardrobe, a piano, a bed you cannot shift on your own, or a sofa that blocks the door should stay where it is. Do not drag it. Say what has to remain when you ask for a quote. Working around a piece of furniture is normal. Finding that out on the morning is what uses up the day.

Pictures and mirrors come down if you can lift them without a stretch you are unsure of. Leave the hooks. A hook pulled out of old plaster makes a bigger hole than the one already there, and filling is part of making the wall good. Fixed shelves stay unless they lift straight off.

What not to take apart

  • Do not remove sockets, switches, light fittings or radiators. A live fitting is not evening preparation.
  • Do not lift floorboards, move a boiler, or drain anything.
  • Do not wash the walls with bleach, or with a cleaner you would not put on your hands. A wall that is already dry and free of cobwebs is enough. If there is grease above a cooker, say so.
  • Do not put a coat of paint on yourself to save time. A soft new coat, or a tin that does not suit the surface, adds work.
  • Do not strip wallpaper unless that has already been agreed. Paper often brings plaster with it. If the paper is to come off, say so in the message. The wallpapering page is the work of hanging it once the wall is ready, not a reason to strip the house yourself the week before.

You do not need to buy dust sheets or mask the house. On the interior painting page, the work includes floors, furniture and fittings covered before a tin is opened, and the room left tidy at the end of the day. Clearing the clutter is the part that helps. Covering the room is the decorating.

The way in, and who is in the house

The hall is the way into the room, and in many of these houses the hall, stairs and landing are the job. Clear coats, shoes, prams and bags off the route from the door you want used. If the stairs are being painted, anything stored on the steps needs to move before the first morning.

Keep dogs and cats out of the room while paint is wet. A door left open for a cat is how hair gets into a finish. Keeping children out of that room is the same ordinary care. You do not need to empty the house. Do say if someone is sleeping in the next room, or if a dog will be loose in the hall.

Streets are often tight. Fintona’s own page says so of the village, and a space near the door helps on an older terrace in Omagh too, if you can leave one. It depends on the street. Name the town either way: Omagh, Strabane, Enniskillen, Irvinestown, Kesh, or a village in between. The same clearing applies in each of them.

Heat, fresh air, and a wet patch

In the colder months a spare room is often the one left unheated. If the heating is already on in the house, leave that room on the same setting as the rooms you live in. Do not stand a gas heater or a borrowed stove in a closed bedroom to hurry it along. Do not light an open fire or a stove hard against a wall that is about to be painted. A window that opens is enough air. Shut it if the house will be empty.

If a patch is cold, stained or wet to the touch, do not seal it with your own paint the day before, and do not expect paper over it in the morning. That includes a ground-floor wall in an older house, the sort of caution already noted for places such as Ballinamallard. Show the patch. Paint and paper do not fix a leak. The repairs page is plain about the limit: this is making good for decoration, not a damp-proof course.

Choose the colour before the morning

Winter light in Tyrone and Fermanagh is flat. A colour that looked right on a phone often dies on a north-facing wall in Omagh or Enniskillen. If you are unsure, put a sample on the actual wall and look at it in daylight and again with the lights on. The interior painting page says the same, because the choice is yours before the room is committed.

If you have already bought the paint, give the name. If you have not, say which rooms, and whether the doors, frames and skirting are included. A message that says “walls only” should mean walls only. Woodwork left undone is why a freshly painted room can still look tired.

Also say what is wrong with the surface: paper still on the wall, a ceiling with old filler, a stain, a dark colour going pale, or cracks over a door. What those things do to the work is in how much it costs to paint a room. This note does not add a price. Photographs of the room as it is, sent on WhatsApp, are more useful than a tidy picture of the fireplace.

What to put on the quote

The quote form asks for the town, the type of job and a rough timescale. The message is for the rooms, whether paper has to come off, and anything that should be made good first. A house in Lisnaskea or Cookstown needs the town written in. So does a street in Omagh.

A shop, an office or a house between tenants is a different sort of preparation. Say the opening hours, or the date the next tenant is due. That is what the commercial decorating page already asks for. Moving a sofa is the household version of the same idea: say what cannot be in the way.

On the morning, a clear path, the colour decided, pets out of the room, and a note of anything that has to stay will do. The walls are the decorating.
