Halls, kitchens and ceilings

The job that comes up most for an interior painter in Omagh is the hall, stairs and landing. It is the part every visitor sees, and it takes the knocks. The usual brief is wallpaper above a dado, woodwork that has gone yellow, and spindles that have never had a clean finish. Paper on that top half is wallpapering. Finish, light, and a wall that should wait

Light across Tyrone and Fermanagh is flat for much of the year. A colour picked from a phone often looks duller on a north-facing wall than it did on the screen. If you are unsure, put a sample on the actual wall and look at it in the morning and again in the evening before the whole room is committed. Late autumn is a useful week for that check, because you are seeing the light the room has for most of the year.

Walls are usually matt. Doors, skirting and banisters are usually a wipeable eggshell or satin, because hands touch them. A dark colour going pale, or a water stain, takes a primer and more coats, and that belongs in the quote. If the paint is already bought, give the name of the tin and say whether you are supplying it. A lower figure with the paint left out is a different job.

A patch that is still damp is a reason to wait. Mould and condensation marks come back if the cause is still feeding them, and ordinary emulsion will not stop that.
